Overview

Abstract & Core Research Question

Looped and recurrent language models (such as ByteDance/Ouro-1.4B-Thinking) scale reasoning depth by repeatedly applying a shared transformer block $F_{\theta,t}$ to a continuous latent state $H_t \in \mathbb{R}^{L \times d}$ across $T$ recurrent iterations before decoding tokens. However, a single deterministic trajectory ($S_0$) can become trapped in a suboptimal basin during intermediate recurrent steps, while naive parallel sampling ($P_0$) runs isolated trajectories that never share latent transition geometry before readout.

We ask: Can a recurrent reasoning trajectory recover from intermediate basin traps by briefly visiting the latent neighborhood of a perturbed companion trajectory, evaluating the shared transition operator $F_{\theta,t}$ along the cross-trajectory midpoint, and importing the resulting local update vector back into its own state?

We introduce TEBDİL (Cooperative Latent Excursions for Recurrent Reasoning), a training-free, zero-parameter inference-time intervention. At step $t{=}1$, TEBDİL forks a full-sequence norm-bounded perturbed donor trajectory $H_1^B$. At step $t{=}2$, it constructs the midpoint $H_2^{\mathrm{visit}} = (1-\alpha)H_2^A + \alpha H_2^B$, evaluates the shared recurrent block once at $H_2^{\mathrm{visit}}$ to extract the local transition vector $V_2^{\mathrm{visit}} = F_{\theta,2}(H_2^{\mathrm{visit}}) - H_2^{\mathrm{visit}}$, clips its relative Frobenius norm to a trust region, and updates the primary branch via $H_3^{A'} = H_2^A + (1-\beta)V_2^A + \beta \tilde{V}_2^{\mathrm{visit}}$. Across 634 real model trace executions, TEBDİL demonstrates that: (1) full-sequence perturbation ($\eta{=}0.25$) overcomes the $10\times$ single-step contraction of Ouro's 96 Sandwich RMSNorm layers; (2) evaluating $F_{\theta,2}$ at the midpoint generates substantial nonlinear excursion curvature $\|C_2\|_F = 42.06$ that is absent in linear update mixing ($M_1$); and (3) our label-free adaptive policy ($E_2$) improves held-out test accuracy by +5.56% over $S_0$/$P_0$ with 0 degradations, including emergent cooperative rescues where both independent branches fail.

Architecture & Formulation

TEBDİL at a Glance

Unlike multi-agent systems that pass text or train external adapter modules, TEBDİL operates entirely inside a single frozen looped language model by exploiting the nonlinear vector field of its shared recurrent transition operator $F_{\theta,t}$.

Part 1

Overcoming Sandwich RMSNorm Contraction in Ouro-1.4B

In ByteDance/Ouro-1.4B-Thinking, each Universal Transformer recurrent step $t \in \{0,1,2,3\}$ passes the hidden state $H_t \in \mathbb{R}^{L \times 2048}$ through all 24 shared decoder layers followed by final RMSNorm. Each decoder layer applies 4 Sandwich RMSNorm operations (input_layernorm, post_attention_layernorm, post_feedforward_layernorm, plus residual scaling), totaling 96 RMSNorm normalizations per recurrent step.

Last-Token Scope ($\eta \le 0.05$) Contracted $10\times$ in 1 UT step

Perturbing only the final token position leaves all $L{-}1$ prefix tokens identical. Self-attention immediately averages over the unperturbed prefix, contracting $\|H_2^A - H_2^B\|_F / \|H_2^A\|_F$ to $<0.005$ with curvature $\|C_2\|_F = 0.94$ and $0\%$ rescue rate.

Full-Sequence Scope ($\eta = 0.25$, $\tau = 0.35$) Sustained Basin Separation

Applying norm-matched Gaussian noise across all token positions at $t{=}1$ shifts keys and values across the entire context, expanding relative branch distance from $0.250$ at $t{=}1$ to $0.459$ at $t{=}2$ ($\cos(H_2^A, H_2^B) = 0.895$) while remaining $100\%$ finite.

Sandwich RMSNorm Contraction vs. Token Scope. Full-sequence perturbation at $t{=}1$ generates $44\times$ larger nonlinear excursion curvature $\|C_2\|_F$ at $t{=}2$ than last-token perturbation and doubles accuracy on the Phase 3 ablation subset.

Part 2

The Cooperative Latent Excursion Operator ($E_1$)

Step A · Midpoint Probe Cross-Trajectory Interpolation at $t=2$

Given primary state $H_t^A$ and perturbed donor state $H_t^B$, TEBDİL constructs a temporary visit state at fractional depth $\alpha \in [0, 1]$ and evaluates the shared recurrent operator $F_{\theta,t}$:

$$H_t^{\mathrm{visit}} = (1 - \alpha) H_t^A + \alpha H_t^B$$ $$V_t^{\mathrm{visit}} = F_{\theta,t}\!\left(H_t^{\mathrm{visit}},\; x,\; \mathcal{C}_t\right) - H_t^{\mathrm{visit}}$$
Step B · Trust-Region Return Norm-Clipped Directional Import

To prevent representation explosion, $V_t^{\mathrm{visit}}$ is clipped relative to $\|V_t^A\|_F$ ($\gamma_{\max}=1.5$) and blended into Branch $A$'s update with return weight $\beta \in [0, 1]$:

$$\tilde{V}_t^{\mathrm{visit}} = V_t^{\mathrm{visit}} \cdot \min\!\left(1,\; \frac{\gamma_{\max}\|V_t^A\|_F}{\|V_t^{\mathrm{visit}}\|_F + \varepsilon}\right)$$ $$H_{t+1}^{A'} = H_t^A + (1 - \beta) V_t^A + \beta \tilde{V}_t^{\mathrm{visit}}$$
Why Return to $H_t^A$ Instead of Staying at $H_t^{\mathrm{visit}}$ ($C_0$)? In our Phase 3 ablation, staying at the midpoint without returning ($C_0$: $H_{t+1}^{C_0} = H_t^{\mathrm{visit}} + V_t^{\mathrm{visit}}$) shifts the base coordinate origin away from $H_t^A$, losing the rescue on graph_validation_002 ($38.89\%$ vs $E_1$'s $44.44\%$). Anchoring the return at $H_t^A$ preserves primary trajectory semantics while importing only the midpoint's directional velocity.
Part 3

Label-Free Adaptive Excursion Policy ($E_2$)

Unconditional excursion ($E_1$) applies $\beta{=}0.5$ on every problem instance—even when Branch $A$ is already highly confident, risking unnecessary compute or over-excursion. Our adaptive policy $E_2$ projects intermediate state $H_2^A$ through the native readout head $g_\phi(H_2^A)$ at step $t{=}2$ and computes two strictly label-free uncertainty signals:

$$m_t^A = z_{(1)}^A - z_{(2)}^A \quad (\text{Top-2 Logit Margin}), \qquad \mathcal{H}_t^A = -\sum_{v \in V} p_t^A(v)\log p_t^A(v)$$

$E_2$ triggers the midpoint excursion if and only if: (1) primary uncertainty is elevated ($m_2^A \le 0.70$ or $\mathcal{H}_2^A \ge 1.20$), and (2) relative branch divergence lies inside the trust window $\frac{\|H_2^A - H_2^B\|_F}{\|H_2^A\|_F} \in [0.005,\; 0.65]$. Otherwise, $E_2$ skips $F_{\theta,2}(H_2^{\mathrm{visit}})$, saving recurrent block calls and preventing degradation.

Zero-Leakage Verification & Test-Time Efficiency

Enforced by automated unit test tests/test_no_label_leakage.py: AdaptiveExcursionPolicy.decide() accepts only $(H_t^A, H_t^B, \text{logits}_A, \text{logits}_B)$ and is invariant to arbitrary mutations of gold_answer.

Why Midpoint Excursion Beats Linear Mixing

Theoretical & Mechanistic Insights

What mathematically distinguishes TEBDİL ($E_1$) from simply averaging the hidden states ($M_0$) or averaging the individual update vectors ($M_1$) of two trajectories?

Proposition 1 — Exact Curvature Decomposition

Define the nonlinear excursion curvature residual $C_t$ at step $t$ as the deviation of the midpoint transition vector from the convex combination of endpoint transition vectors:

$$C_t \;\triangleq\; V_t^{\mathrm{visit}} - \Big[(1-\alpha)V_t^A + \alpha V_t^B\Big]$$ $$H_{t+1}^{A'}(E_1) \;=\; \underbrace{H_t^A + (1-\alpha\beta)V_t^A + \alpha\beta V_t^B}_{\text{Linear Update Mixing } M_1(\gamma=\alpha\beta)} \;+\; \beta C_t$$
Causal Implication: If $F_{\theta,t}$ were locally affine between $H_t^A$ and $H_t^B$, then $C_t \equiv 0$ and $E_1$ would be algebraically identical to $M_1$. Empirically, $\|C_2\|_F = 42.06$ ($37.1\%$ of $\|V_2^{\mathrm{visit}}\|_F$), and ablating $C_2 \to 0$ eliminates $E_1$'s rescue on graph_validation_002.

Proposition 2 — Directional Specificity of $C_t$

Is the benefit of $C_t$ merely an artifact of injecting extra vector norm at step $t{=}2$? We test two norm-matched directional controls in Phase 3:

$$E_1^{\text{rand-curv}}:\quad V_t^{\text{synth}} = \big[(1-\alpha)V_t^A + \alpha V_t^B\big] + \frac{\|C_t\|_F}{\|u\|_F}\,u$$ $$E_0:\quad H_t^{\text{visit,rand}} = H_t^A + \alpha \|H_t^B - H_t^A\|_F \frac{w}{\|w\|_F}$$
Empirical Proof: Replacing $C_2$ with a norm-matched random vector ($E_1^{\text{rand-curv}}$) drops validation accuracy from $44.44\%$ to $38.89\%$ ($0/11$ rescues). Probing a random direction of equal distance ($E_0$) inflates curvature $4\times$ to $168.41$ and degrades gold log-prob by $-0.1254$.

Pre-Computation & Publication Readiness

Where We Are & Next Steps to Final Paper

All core infrastructure, exact adapter equivalence tests, 12-method control suites, and pilot/test validation runs are complete (~80% to publication). Here is the exact 3-step plan before generating the paper.

Step 1
Scale Test Sample Size ($n = 150\text{--}300$)

Expand held-out evaluation from $n{=}36$ to $150\text{--}300$ instances ($50\text{--}100$ per family across 3 seeds). At $+5.56\%$ effect size, $n \ge 180$ tightens paired bootstrap 95% CIs and powers McNemar's test ($p < 0.05$).

Step 2
Margin-Proportional $\beta(m_t^A, m_t^B)$ Damping

Replace hard binary gating in $E_2$ with smooth confidence weighting $\beta_t = \beta_{\max} \cdot \sigma(m_t^{\mathrm{visit}} - m_t^A)$ to prevent over-excursion on borderline-correct instances like arith_validation_003.

Step 3
Paper Generation & Vector Figures

Compile the LaTeX manuscript (paper/main.tex) with publication-grade PDF/SVG figures generated directly from reports/final_results.json, including theorem proofs and qualitative trace appendices.
